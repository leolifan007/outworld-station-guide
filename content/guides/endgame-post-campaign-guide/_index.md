## You Won. Now What?

Launching the Arkship is the game's narrative finish line, and for a long time it was also the practical one. You had a working station, nothing left to research that mattered, and a factory that ran itself. Reviews hammered this: the endgame was described, fairly, as an idle simulator.

**v1.1.2.0 finally answers the question.** It bolts a real post-campaign loop onto the game: Arkship Terminal missions, a Station Prestige track, and cosmetic rewards that give a finished base something to become. This guide ties all of it together into a routine you can actually run.

{{< callout type="verdict" title="The Short Version" >}}
After the campaign, four activities keep the game alive: **Arkship Terminal missions** (your Prestige income), **Station Prestige levels** (your reward track), **showcase building** (where Decorations go), and **factory cleanup** (the maintenance a long-running station quietly needs). None of them are mandatory, all of them are available, and together they turn a "finished" save into a living one.
{{< /callout >}}

## The Four Post-Campaign Activities

### 1. Arkship Terminal Missions

The terminal becomes a mission board once the campaign ends. It offers **randomly generated missions** that pay **Station Prestige Points**. This is the engine of the entire endgame loop -- it is the only thing that produces Prestige. The full workflow is in the [Arkship Terminal Missions Guide](/guides/arkship-terminal-missions-guide/).

### 2. Station Prestige Levels

Prestige Points raise your **Station Prestige Level**, and each level grants **random rewards** -- confirmed to include new **Decoration Elements** and **Tech Points**. Follow the [Station Prestige Level Guide](/guides/station-prestige-level-guide/) for the reward structure and a levelling routine.

### 3. Showcase Building

Decoration Elements exist to be placed. The reward for finishing the campaign is the freedom to make the station *yours* rather than merely functional. Pick one region -- a residential ring, a command deck, a monument -- and build it out deliberately. This is the closest thing to a creative mode the game offers, and it is worth doing slowly.

### 4. Factory Cleanup

A station that has run for a hundred hours has accumulated debt: lines you retired but never removed, buffers you over-built, throughput you never re-tuned. The post-campaign phase is the natural time to pay that debt down. It is not glamorous, but a clean factory makes everything above run smoother.

## A Working Weekly Routine

You do not need to grind. A relaxed rotation covers everything:

| Session | Focus | Why |
|---------|-------|-----|
| Start of session | Read the terminal board | Missions rotate; check before committing |
| Main block | Clear missions automation can finish alone | Fastest Prestige for the least effort |
| Once banked | Collect + level Prestige | Rewards only roll when you level |
| Wind-down | One cleanup task or decoration pass | Steady improvement over time |

The core insight is that **your factory does the endgame work**. Missions that lean on production you already automated complete themselves. That is by design -- the developers built an idle-friendly loop, so lean into it instead of fighting it with manual effort. If your production lines are healthy per the [endgame production patterns](/guides/endgame-final-stretch-guide/), missions become background income.

## What To Spend Tech Points On

Prestige levels hand out **Tech Points**, which is a quiet gift if you rushed the campaign and left research unfinished -- which most players do. Rather than grinding leftover nodes directly, let mission rewards backfill them.

Prioritise research that unlocks something real: throughput upgrades, new building tiers, quality-of-life unlocks. Skip nodes that only exist to satisfy a completionist urge. The [tech point strategy guide](/guides/tech-point-farming/) covers which nodes actually pay off, and it also helps you sequence what is left.

## The Cleanup Checklist

This is the part nobody writes guides about, so here it is. Run through it once after the campaign:

| Task | Why it matters |
|------|----------------|
| Retire dead production lines | Frees power, space, and mental overhead |
| Re-tune saturated belts | The 540 items/min cap still bites in the late game |
| Audit storage filters | Backed-up buffers are silent throughput killers |
| Verify power margins | Long unattended sessions punish a thin power grid |
| Back up your save | A campaign-length save is worth protecting |

For the storage half of that list, the [overflow sink management guide](/guides/overflow-sink-management-guide/) covers how to stop buffers from strangling a line. For the power half, the multi-source power grid guide explains keeping margins healthy.

Finally, before you rework anything on a save you care about, the [save backup guide](/guides/save-backup-guide/) is a two-minute insurance policy.

## What This Means For The Game's Future

The direction is clear and worth saying plainly. The September 2026 developer update described a system where missions earn "new station parts, resource nodes and other rewards" -- not just cosmetics. If that lands, Prestige stops being decoration money and becomes a second growth curve that feeds your factory.

That would be a genuine answer to the oldest complaint about the genre's endgames: that once the factory is optimal, there is nothing to chase. v1.1.2.0 is the first step; whether it becomes a full second act depends on what the team ships next.

## Bottom Line

Post-campaign Outworld Station in v1.1.2.0 is a loop, not a wall. Run Arkship missions for Prestige, convert levels into Tech Points and Decorations, spend those on research and a showcase build, and keep the factory clean between sessions. Your automation does most of the work -- your job is to check the board, collect, and decide what the station becomes next.
